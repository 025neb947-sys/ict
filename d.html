Information Security

Information security (InfoSec) is the practice of protecting digital and physical data from unauthorized access, use, disclosure, disruption, modification, or destruction. It ensures the confidentiality, integrity, and availability (CIA triad) of information through policies, technology, and procedures, encompassing both digital cybersecurity and physical security measures.
Key Principles: The CIA Triad
Confidentiality: Ensuring only authorized users access data.
Integrity: Maintaining the accuracy and trustworthiness of data by preventing unauthorized modifications.
Availability: Guaranteeing that data is accessible to authorized users when needed.

Key Components & Security Measures
Technical Controls: Firewalls, encryption, antivirus software, and multi-factor authentication (MFA).
Organizational Policies: Risk assessment, employee training, and security protocols.
Physical Security: Protecting hardware, data centers, and devices from theft or damage.

Common Threats
Human Error: Weak passwords, lost devices, or falling for phishing scams.
Cyber Attacks: Ransomware, malware, and sophisticated phishing attacks.
Insider Threats: Employees or partners misusing access.
Legacy Systems: Vulnerable, outdated software and hardware.

Main Types of Information Security
Application Security: Protecting software and APIs.
Cloud Security: Defending data within cloud environments.
Data Security: Protecting data at rest and in transit.
Network Infrastructure Security: Securing network traffic and devices.
